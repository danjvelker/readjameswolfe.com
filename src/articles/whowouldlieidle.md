Do you live what you profess?

Any Christian would (or should) profess that faith in Jesus Christ is the most important aspect of their life, that all other hopes and goals are as good as worthless when held in comparison to glorifying him. But is that how most of us functionally live?

Most of us are distracted. Many of us are forgetful. A great deal of us know how we ought to live, but sin or weakness or fear or love of comfort tend to hold us back. In my experience this problem doesn’t tend to originate from a lack of knowledge, but from misplaced affections. We become what we behold, and most of us spend far more time beholding our own loves than we behold Christ. 

In the end, there tends to be a gap between what we believe and how we live. I like to think of this gap in terms of professed belief and functional belief.

* Professed belief includes the things we think and say. These could be intellectual convictions, or what most of the time we just call theology. We believe that Christ died for our sins, we believe that we are saved by grace and not works, and we believe that our work here on earth is to grow in sanctification and preach the gospel to others no matter the cost. We would say (profess) that we “believe” these things.
* Functional belief describes how we actually live. These are often at odds with our intellectual convictions, our theology. We may believe that Christ has saved us, but we act like we still owe a debt to sin. We believe that we’re saved by grace alone, but we act like we still have something left to contribute. We believe that proclaiming the gospel is the defining source of meaning in our lives, but we’re timid when it comes to actually living it out. We profess one thing, but functionally our lives reveal that we’re governed by different beliefs.

We profess Christ crucified. In him, we have an invincible salvation secured by impenetrable grace. We look to him, the founder and perfecter of our faith, as our great high priest and eternal advocate before the Father, who endlessly intercedes on our behalf. We believe that Jesus is our glorious representative, mighty king, worthy of our worship, and a perfect friend.

Do you treat him that way?

*“Who would lie idle when the king has returned?”*

I don’t. Often, my prayers are halfhearted, routine, chore-like and dull. My obedience (when it comes) often appeals to the “cosmic slot machine,” who issues rewards in return for good behavior. I do read my Bible each day – for about ten minutes, before I devote hours to mindless entertainment and consumeristic hedonism. I serve in ways that I’m comfortable with, and lie to excuse myself from things that would be mildly uncomfortable. Some friend I am.

Do our churches look like we actually believe in Christ crucified?

I’m not sure. What can you say when your theology is terrific but people are scared to pray in a small group? What do we communicate about Christ and our faith when we treat Christ’s Bride, his holy church, more as a commodity for our benefit than as a beauty to glorify and devote ourselves to? Invincible salvation? Please. We’re scared of sounding dumb in small groups. It feels like most people are uncomfortable to pray, serve, or lead. It feels like everyone is waiting for someone else to step up and do the work. It feels like most of us who claim Christ spend all day unearthing reasons to excuse ourselves from actually… you know, living out what we believe. Busyness, comfort, introversion, sleep schedules, family game nights, “that’s just not what we do.”

We don’t feel like the people that we could be.

There’s work to be done.

Most of us know the work, and we know that it starts inside us, not outside. The work is to pray without ceasing, to preach the gospel with every fiber of your being, to grow in sanctification, to devote yourself to a vision of life in which Christ crucified casts a shadow over every other part of your life, soul, and being.

The work is to read, to worship, to build, to serve, to love. The work is to be with Jesus, live like Jesus, and love like Jesus. But if you’re going to do that you have to know who he is. A lack of devotion won’t be solved by increased intellectualism, but you won’t grow far without putting in the work.

*“Who would lie idle when the king has returned?”*

We are trained to be in Christian spaces far better than we are trained to be Christian people. Many of us think that growing in our faith equals serving more and more until all our capacity is filled and our lives are busy and we’re still stuck in horrible sins and ugly patterns of thought and we don’t actually love Jesus any more and we’re just tired and empty and all our efforts feel meaningless but at least we grew in our faith. Right?

A deficient view of Jesus will impoverish your life. A deficient view of Jesus explains both why you find yourself growing in a negative direction (towards sin), and why you feel stunted in your efforts to grow positively (towards holiness). Your understanding of the “good-news” gospel of Christ profoundly shapes your ability to live boldly, fight sin, flee the over-love of comfort, and be transformed.

Note what your gospel understanding doesn’t directly correlate to. It doesn’t equip you to serve in the nursery, or to go on a short-term missions trip, or to post more Bible verses to your Pinterest page. It doesn’t mean that you’ll wear a cross necklace or dress more modestly or never say anything spicier than “gosh darning heck.” You can have an agonizingly deficient view of Jesus while performing perfectly in Christian spaces.

Nothing would give your Enemy more pleasure.

So give up on yourself! Give away everything you ever wanted, every desire and ambition and idolatry and excuse. You will never possess more than when you’ve given everything away and have nothing to hold but Christ. You profess Christ crucified; be crucified with him. You profess yourself a sinner saved only by grace; maybe start to act like it.

Will you continue to lie idle when the king has returned? The hour has come for you to wake from sleep. The night is far gone, the day is at hand – so throw back everything you were clinging to and put on Christ!  Seek him boldly in prayer, with your service, with the time you can spare and the time you can’t. Seek him with every muscle in your body and every ounce of tissue in your heart until you ache from your weak, foolish, unadorned love, and all your mind and heart radiate with his presence, and every deepest dream and desire turns to him and him alone, and all the things of this earth fade away and become like dust in your mouth.

So be the person your belief describes. Live what you profess. Rise to the work before you. Become what you behold, and behold Christ above all else. Be with Jesus, live like Jesus, and love like Jesus.

*“My lord, you called me. I come. Who would lie idle when the king has returned?”*
